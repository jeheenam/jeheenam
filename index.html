Research

A map of a fly, one molecule at a time

Ye He Lab, CUNY Neuroscience Initiative

Student Researcher and Data Analyst, 9th grade to present

I do spatial metabolomics: I section whole fruit flies, image them by MALDI mass spectrometry, and analyze where each metabolite appears across the body using SCiLS Lab and other mass-spectrometry software. From that work I built an independent whole-body metabolomics atlas of the fly, and I am now extending it to aging, comparing the metabolic maps of adult flies at different ages to see where the chemistry changes first.

I also annotate images and data for two larger projects in the lab, on adult fly aging and on larval spinal cord injury, which is how I learned the full workflow from sample preparation to spectral processing and interpretation.

Summer Science Program, Bacterial Genomics

Summer 2026, five weeks, three-person research team

Our team asked whether the density of a bacterial culture at the moment it meets an antibiotic changes how much antibiotic it takes for resistance to evolve. We grew Vibrio natriegens in a turbidostat over seven days, raising kanamycin concentrations while holding each culture to a set density, then sequenced the evolved populations. Cultures kept at higher densities needed more kanamycin before resistance appeared, and sequencing pointed to the fusA gene.

Read the abstract

Population density and the evolution of kanamycin resistance in Vibrio natriegens

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) has emerged as one of the most pressing global public health threats, driven by the widespread misuse of antimicrobial drugs that imposes strong selective pressures on bacterial populations. Antibiotic resistance, a major subclass of AMR, arises when bacteria acquire genetic changes that enable them to survive antibiotic treatment, making infections increasingly difficult to treat. Although the aminoglycoside antibiotic kanamycin is extensively studied for its mechanism of action and applications in molecular genetics, the influence of bacterial population density on the evolution of kanamycin resistance remains poorly understood. This study investigated whether the optical density (OD) threshold of Vibrio natriegens cultures at the time of antibiotic exposure influences the concentration of kanamycin required for resistance to evolve. It was hypothesized that cultures maintained at higher OD thresholds before antibiotic exposure would require higher concentrations of kanamycin before resistance emerged. To test this hypothesis, V. natriegens cultures were grown in a turbidostat over a seven-day period, where culture density was regulated by dilution at predetermined OD thresholds while kanamycin concentrations were progressively increased. Bacterial growth and adaptation were continuously monitored, and whole-genome sequencing was performed on evolved populations to identify mutations associated with resistance. Cultures exposed to kanamycin at higher OD thresholds evolved resistance only after reaching higher kanamycin concentrations. Genomic analysis identified mutations in the fusA gene among resistant populations, supporting its role in kanamycin resistance, while no mutations were detected in rpsB or rpsE. These findings demonstrate that bacterial population density at the onset of antibiotic exposure influences the evolutionary trajectory of kanamycin resistance in V. natriegens, providing new insight into how factors shape antimicrobial resistance evolution and informing future strategies to mitigate the emergence of antibiotic-resistant bacteria.

Keywords: antibiotic resistance, kanamycin, Vibrio natriegens, fusA

The Wait

A documentary, and a card patients can hand to the front desk

Producer, Young Global Leaders of the East (YGLE), 2025 to present

The Wait is a student documentary about Korean immigrants who avoid going to the hospital because of the language barrier, and who let illness grow while they wait. I produce the film, which means managing its schedule and interviews, and I coordinate the bilingual card that goes with it.

The card is a Korean and English patient card for hospital and dental visits. A patient can point to why she came, where it hurts, which medications she takes, and that she needs an interpreter, which federal law entitles her to at no cost. A registered nurse reviewed the medical text and a dentist reviewed the dental text in September 2026. Our team printed 300 and donated all of them to Korean American community organizations in New Jersey and New York; 500 more are planned for New York and Connecticut.

On October 17, 2026, I ran our card booth at the Tenafly Korean Community Center with two teammates. We spoke with Tenafly’s mayor, and interpreter services, senior support groups, and a law firm asked us for cards.

Journalism

Four years of hard news, then the masthead

The Echo, Tenafly High School

Co-Editor-in-Chief, 12th grade; Copy Editor, 11th grade; reporter since 9th

I have written and researched hard-news stories nearly every week since freshman year, mostly on social issues, with opinion and school-life pieces in between. As Copy Editor I edited five or more articles a week and compiled the weekly Stories of the Week email that goes to middle and high school students and staff.

When New Jersey’s school cellphone ban made it hard for reporters to conduct interviews and take photos, I introduced press passes so they could keep working. As Co-Editor-in-Chief I run meetings, set agendas, and oversee publication. Since 10th grade I have also co-hosted a course-selection podcast interviewing teachers and students, and I was selected through the paper to mentor elementary school newspaper students every week, helping them brainstorm, edit, and get their work published.
